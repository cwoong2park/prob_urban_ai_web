About

Why PUAI exists

A forecast can tell us how busy a street might become. A predictive distribution gives us a way to ask how much the outcome might vary and whether the available evidence is strong enough to act on.

PUAI is a one-person independent research initiative at the intersection of probabilistic machine learning, spatial analytics, and urban planning. Its work connects model development with the decisions those models are intended to support.

Blog

Research notes

What does a predictive distribution tell us?

Imagine two street segments with the same predicted hourly footfall. The range of plausible counts may be wider on one street, for example because it has fewer observations or more variable demand.

A predictive distribution lets us ask a question tied to an action: how likely is footfall to exceed the threshold at which we need temporary traffic controls? Before using that probability, we can check whether similarly confident forecasts have been reliable on held-out streets and times.

How do we check a prediction interval?

For a nominal 90% prediction interval, start by measuring how often held-out observations fall inside it. Then look at interval widths. Coverage near 90% tells you little about usefulness if the intervals are too wide for the decision at hand.

Citywide averages can conceal systematic problems. Check coverage and width by neighbourhood, time of day, or street type where the sample supports it.
